\section{The accumulator as a bet, and a calibration test built on the boundary}
\label{sec:evalue}

\paragraph{What is assumed here, and by whom.} Everything Section~\ref{sec:forfeit} proves is
deterministic and pathwise. Proposition~2's window $|E_T| \le c\,h(T)+1$, and the boundaries
(\ref{eq:boundaries}) that locate its edge, hold on every trajectory a legal adapted adversary can
produce; no distributional model on the data appears in either statement, and neither is a
statement about a null hypothesis. This section neither uses that nor weakens it. It runs the
other way: it takes the error sequence a deployment actually produced as \emph{data}, and tests a
hypothesis about it. The hypothesis is imposed here, for the test alone, with $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$
the history through round $t-1$ (scores, deployed thresholds and errors) against which $\hat{q}_t$
is already required to be predictable:
\[
  H_0: \qquad \mathrm{err}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1} \;\sim\; \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\alpha)
  \quad\text{for every } t ,
\]
and it says that the shipped intervals were conditionally calibrated at the target rate. $H_0$ is
the object under test. It is not a premise of Proposition~2, of (\ref{eq:boundaries}), or of any
other claim in this paper; nothing above requires it, implies it, or is disturbed if it fails.
When it fails, what loses validity is the test below and nothing else. We are explicit about this
because the rest of the paper assumes no distribution at all, and a reader who meets a Bernoulli
null on this page must not read it backwards into the pages before it.

\paragraph{The accumulator is a capital process, and it is signed.} A Skeptic who each round pays
$\alpha$ for the payoff $\mathrm{err}_t$ has cumulative net gain exactly $E_t$, so this paper's
accumulator is a game-theoretic capital process at unit stake
\citep{shafer2019gtfpf,ramdas2023savi}. It is not an e-value and not an e-process: it is signed,
it goes negative on exactly the trajectories the mirror boundary $\tau^{\star-}$ governs, and
Proposition~2 bounds it pathwise by feedback rather than under a null by Ville's inequality
\citep{vovk2021evalues}. Nothing below proposes $E_t$, or a transform of it, as a certificate for
any claim this paper makes. What is formal is the standard transform, and it is invoked rather
than invented.

\paragraph{The test martingale, and why it is one.} Fix $\lambda$ and set $M_0 = 1$,
\[
  M_t \;=\; \prod_{i \le t}\big(1 + \lambda(\mathrm{err}_i - \alpha)\big) .
\]
Each factor takes one of two values, $1 + \lambda(1-\alpha)$ on a miss and $1 - \lambda\alpha$ on a
cover, so $M_t \ge 0$ for every path if and only if $\lambda \in [-1/(1-\alpha), 1/\alpha]$, which
for $\alpha = 0.1$ is $[-10/9, 10]$. Under $H_0$, $\mathbb{E}[\mathrm{err}_t \mid
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = \alpha$, so each factor is conditionally mean one and $\mathbb{E}[M_t \mid
\mathcal{F}_{t-1}] = M_{t-1}$: $M_t$ is a nonnegative martingale started at $1$, i.e.\ a test
martingale for $H_0$. Ville's inequality then gives $\mathbb{P}_{H_0}(\sup_t M_t \ge 1/\eta) \le
\eta$, so ``reject $H_0$ the first time $M_t \ge 1/\eta$'' is an anytime-valid level-$\eta$ test of
the deployed intervals' calibration, at any stopping time and with no horizon fixed in advance.
That is a test of the deployment, not of Section~\ref{sec:forfeit}.

\paragraph{The boundary transfers exactly, because $M_t$ sees only $(t, E_t)$.} Since
$\mathrm{err}_i \in \{0,1\}$, the product collapses. Writing $N_t$ for the miss count and using
$N_t = \alpha t + E_t$,

\begin{gather*}
  \log M_t \;=\; K(\lambda)\,t \;+\; L(\lambda)\,E_t, \\
  K(\lambda) = \alpha\log\!\big(1+\lambda(1-\alpha)\big) + (1-\alpha)\log(1-\lambda\alpha), \\
  L(\lambda) = \log\frac{1+\lambda(1-\alpha)}{1-\lambda\alpha} .
\end{gather*}
with $K(\lambda) \le 0$, $K(\lambda) = 0$ only at $\lambda = 0$, and $\operatorname{sign}
L(\lambda) = \operatorname{sign}\lambda$. So $M_t$ depends on the path only through the horizon and
the accumulator -- the same statistic Section~\ref{sec:forfeit}'s dichotomy is stated in -- and the
transfer is an identity rather than the first-order expansion $\log M_t = \lambda E_t +
O(\lambda^2 t)$. It transfers as a dichotomy, not as a measured rate. Inside the retained band
$|E_t| \le c\,h(t)+1$ is logarithmic, so $\log M_t \le K(\lambda)t + |L(\lambda)|(c\,h(t)+1)$ at
every round. Since $K(\lambda) < 0$ the right side falls to $-\infty$ linearly, so $M_T \to 0$
exponentially; and since it is continuous with a single interior maximum it has a finite supremum
$S(\lambda)$ over all $t$, so $\sup_t M_t \le e^{S(\lambda)}$ \emph{uniformly in the horizon}. The
test therefore never rejects at any level $\eta < e^{-S(\lambda)}$, however long the deployment
runs. It is the uniform bound and not the limit that non-rejection needs, because Ville's
inequality prices the supremum. Past either boundary $|E_T|$ is linear in $T$, so some fixed
$\lambda$ of the sign of $E_T$ makes $\log M_T$ grow linearly and the test rejects at any fixed
level within $O(1)$ rounds. Both signs are needed, and that is the point of the two-sided form:
the $\tau^{\star+}$ failure drives $E_T$ up and calls for $\lambda > 0$, the mirror failure at
$\tau^{\star-}$ drives it down and calls for $\lambda < 0$.

\paragraph{What this adds, and what it does not.} It adds a diagnostic and a reading, not a result.
The reading is that $E_t$ is the Skeptic's capital and that Section~\ref{sec:forfeit} therefore
locates, in a quantity a bettor already cares about, the exact width at which a dead band turns a
calibrated deployment into one that an anytime-valid test convicts within a handful of rounds. The
diagnostic is runnable online by a practitioner who has the errors and not the proof. Neither
statement is a claim about $E_t$'s status: $E_t$ is not an e-process, $M_t$ is a textbook transform
imposed on top of the result rather than a part of it, and this paper's contribution is the
tightness of someone else's admissible radius, proved pathwise. $M_t$ is itself an e-value, and we
say so: an e-value for $H_0$ alone, a hypothesis about one deployment's error sequence, imposed on
this page and nowhere else in the paper. No result of this paper is an e-value result or rests on
one. Betting enters online conformal inference elsewhere as an optimiser rather than as evidence
\citep{podkopaev2024betting}.
