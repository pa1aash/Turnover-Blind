\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{The producer.} A forecaster fit on data before $t$ induces a score $s_t$, a
threshold $q_t$ is emitted before $y_t$ is revealed, the deployed set is $C_t = \{y : s_t(x_t,y)
\le q_t\}$ and $\mathrm{err}_t = \mathbf{1}\{s_t(x_t,y_t) > q_t\}$; the target is long-run
coverage, $T^{-1}\sum_{t \le T}\mathrm{err}_t = \alpha + o(1)$, with no model on the data.
Conformal PID \citep{angelopoulos2023pid} manipulates the threshold on the score scale,
\begin{equation}
  q_{t+1} \;=\; \hat{q}_{t+1} \;+\; r_t\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i \le t}(\mathrm{err}_i - \alpha)\Big),
  \label{eq:pid}
\end{equation}
their iteration~(5), in which $r_t$ saturates: condition~(4) requires $|r_t(x)| \ge b$ with the
sign of $x$ once $|x| \ge c\,h(t)$, for $h$ nonnegative, nondecreasing and sublinear. Writing $E_t
= \sum_{i \le t}(\mathrm{err}_i-\alpha)$, Proposition~2 gives $|E_T| \le c\,h(T)+1$; boundedness
is read on realised scores.

\paragraph{One scalar, two readouts, two places to put it.} Damping the movement of a
sequentially updated scalar has two standard readouts, both prior work: a quadratic cost gives linear partial
adjustment towards the target, $\tilde{q}_{t+1} = (1-\lambda)\hat{q}^{\,\mathrm{raw}}_{t+1} +
\lambda\tilde{q}_t$ \citep{garleanu2013dynamic}, and a proportional cost a no-trade band, moving
only when the target is more than $\tau$ away and then pulling back by exactly $\tau$
\citep{constantinides1986,davis1990}. The first is published as post-processing
\citep{godahewa2025stability} and applied to a deployed conformal threshold
\citep[Eq.~13]{binny2025whomoved}. It has two positions in (\ref{eq:pid}), drawn in Figure~\ref{fig:placements}: \textbf{(B)} in the
$\hat{q}_{t+1}$ slot, where both readouts keep $\{\hat{q}_t\}$ in the convex hull of $\hat{q}_1$
and the raw scorecaster's range, inside Corollary~2's admissible set; and \textbf{(A)} downstream
of the completed $q_{t+1}$, outside it. Placement~A is measured here. \emph{The dead band (L1) is not a
stylistic alternative to the partial-adjustment (L2) form}; it is the specific perturbation family
whose failure \S\ref{sec:forfeit} characterises. Neither is safe by construction: L2 forfeits
Proposition~2's rate while covering, L1 past $\tau^{\star}$ loses coverage, and at the legal
$\hat{q}_t \equiv -b/2$ both lose it. What is tight is the radius, not a form.

\paragraph{The quantity, and the names it already has.} We report the deployed threshold's
\emph{travel}, $\mathcal{T}_H = \sum_{t=2}^{H}|q_t-q_{t-1}|$, after actuator travel in process
control. The arithmetic is not new: $\sum|\Delta q|$ over a predictive distribution is the
Multi-Quantile Change \citep[v3, \S3.4.1]{zanotti2026smqc}; verification calls it
\emph{jumpiness}, a \emph{convergence index} and \emph{(in)consistency}
\citep{zsoter2009jumpiness,ehret2010convergence,pappenberger2011consistency}; applied control,
IACER \citep{sarhadi2025criteria}; dynamic-regret online learning, a path length.
Table~\ref{tab:bridge}'s caption names ten across four vocabularies, which is why a
fresh one is used here.

\paragraph{Harness.} $\alpha = 0.1$, $b = 2$, $c = 1$, $h(t) = \log(t+2)$, and the clipped
saturator $r_t(x) = b\,\mathrm{clip}(x/(c\,h(t)),-1,1)$, which meets condition~(4) with equality.
The scorecaster is null and legal, reducing (\ref{eq:pid}) to the pure error integration
Proposition~2 is stated about. Neither free choice is neutral: condition~(4) is a lower bound this saturator meets exactly, and Theorem~1
permits scorecasters $b/2$ either side of the null one. The adversary is specified rather than
tie-broken: it plays $s_t = \mathrm{clip}(\tilde{q}_t+\varepsilon,\pm b/2)$, $\varepsilon =
10^{-9}$, against the \emph{deployed} $\tilde{q}_t$ --- the $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ reading of
``sets the score at the threshold'', firing the strict indicator. Legality is Theorem~1's
$[-b/2,b/2]$ on both sides, so $b/2+b/2 = b$ is exactly tight.
