\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\citet{zaffran2022aci} price ACI's learning rate in the \emph{level}: Theorem~3.1 gives
$\mathbb{E}_{\pi_\gamma}[L] = L_0 + \frac{1}{2}Q''(1-\alpha)\gamma\alpha(1-\alpha) + O(\gamma^{3/2})$, a
statement about \emph{mean length}, which integrates the path away; we hold it fixed.
\citet{min2026questioning} also call coverage and length insufficient, but their interval stability
(Def.~4.1), $\mathbb{E}_X[\mathrm{Var}_{A \mid X, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{ca}}}(| C_{1-\alpha}(X)
|)]$, varies over the algorithm's own randomness given test point \emph{and} calibration set and is
``zero for deterministic methods by design'': a deterministic interval oscillating daily scores exactly zero.
\citet{zhou2025inverse} vary the coverage \emph{level} along a miscoverage--regret trade-off; we fix coverage
and vary the path.
\citet{ryan2026conformalkelly} charges the growth loss to estimation variance passing through the nonlinear
Kelly sizing map, hedged as ``a structural explanation consistent with the results'', and already reports the
daily $\mathrm{sd}(\Delta \log q)$ ($0.00343$ vs.\ $0.00391$) and matched gross leverage
($1.957$--$1.959$); what he does not match is width, nor phrase it as turnover.
\citet{wang2026spoturnover} report that ``[i]ncreasing risk aversion does not meaningfully reduce turnover''
and damp the \emph{weight} path by a heuristic $\delta = 0.1$ partial adjustment, never swept; their paper
carries no interval, coverage or quantile object.
\citet{zhu2026stayorswitch} charge the \emph{decision} for moving; \citet{jia2026portfolio} read a
VaR off an adaptive conformal bound; \citet{lin2024cio} calibrate inverse-optimisation decisions.

\citet{godahewa2025stability} already publish the linear partial-adjustment readout
$\tilde{y} = (1-w_s)\hat{y}_{\mathrm{new}} + w_s\tilde{y}_{\mathrm{prev}}$ as model-agnostic
post-processing, and \citet{genov2026switching} already bound a decision's switching cost by
$\beta L_M \sum_t \| \hat{y}_t - \hat{y}_{t-v} \|$ through a Lipschitz readout map, so we
claim no novelty for either form or for the formulation; switching-cost online learning
\citep{kalai2005lazy,geulen2010dartboard,andrew2013twometrics,chen2018obd} and both maps --- quadratic cost
giving partial adjustment \citep{garleanu2013dynamic}, proportional cost a no-trade band
\citep{constantinides1986,davis1990} --- are prior work, not claims.
\citet{vanbelle2026stabilizing} pin CRPS across arms ($2.91/1.43/0.83$ vs.\ $2.91/1.44/0.83$,
``indistinguishable in terms of forecast quality''), vary $W_1$ between consecutive updates threefold, and
price it through a newsvendor; but their functional is over forecast \emph{revisions} of a fixed target, they
carry no interval and no coverage object, and the effect is $+0.00\%$ in their single-horizon arm.
Closest on the conformal side, \citet{lade2026bcaci} place an exponentially weighted smoother and a
dead-zone in an ACI pipeline but keep both off the width mechanism, and their coverage proposition
turns on that: they secure the monotonicity their proof needs by modifying only the interval centre
and the score computation, leaving the quantile-based width mechanism alone. Their own statement of
that monotonicity's direction is internally inconsistent, and their guarantee is an almost-sure
Robbins--Monro limit, so we read it as corroboration. The placement used here is conceded likewise
--- \citet{angelopoulos2023pid} state coverage for any admissible integrator and any scorecaster and
run a smoothing-family scorecaster themselves, \citet{dupuy2026relevance} give the generic argument
in their Appendix~A, and \citet{duerst2024calibrating} already constrain a Conformal PID scorecaster's
width path --- and what we claim is the exchange it exposes.

Elsewhere in the loop \citet{zheng2025scdsplit}, \citet{wu2025eci}, \citet{chen2023ipoc} and
\citet{dupuy2026relevance} each reach an obstacle and stop, and \citet{binny2025whomoved} state
safety only at the fixed point where their smoothing weight is inert. Our negative statements are
scoped to their instrument: in arXiv title-and-abstract metadata as of August~2026, ``conformal''
pairs with none of ``forecast stability'', ``forecast instability'', ``forecast congruence'',
``jumpiness'' or ``forecast revision'', in either direction, while a body-text index does return
methods-level occupants that a metadata index does not.
