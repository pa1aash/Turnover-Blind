\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{One construction, one adversary, one saturator, and $0.63/(1-w)$ is specific to its
\emph{level}} rather than to its shape, and condition~(4) bounds that level only from below. \textbf{The
inherited guarantee is weak where it applies.} Under the tangent integrator $h(t) = t/\log t$, so
the certified gap $(c\,h(T)+1)/T$ decays as $O(1/\log T)$ rather than $O(1/T)$: what is forfeited
was not a tight certificate. \textbf{The dead-band widths are absolute, and the failure is total
only against the adversary.} $\tau$ is in score units with $b = 2$, so the failing $\tau = 1.5$ is
$0.75\,b$; under i.i.d.\ scores that arm returns $0.249376$ rather than $1.000000$. What survives
is the mechanism: a band wider than $\tau^{\star}$ caps the reachable threshold below the reachable
score range. \textbf{The harness was rebuilt against numbers already read}, which carries less
weight than a rerun done without sight of them; the free choices are registered in the code.
\textbf{Placement~B, the readout Corollary~2 already covers, is not measured here}: it is
conceded rather than tested, and this paper offers no evidence about it beyond the published
argument. \textbf{One seed, and the grid brackets rather than locates.} Every arm is a single
pass at one seed; the three widths per setting bracket each $\tau^{\star}$ but no bisection is
booked, so the edge is located to within a tested interval and not to a digit. \textbf{What
would overturn the tightness claim} is a legal $(\,r_t, \hat{q}_t)$ pair at which a dead band of
width $\tau \le \sup_x r_t(x) + \sup_t \hat{q}_t - b/2$ loses coverage, or one above it that
keeps coverage; the run record contains neither, over the nineteen configurations it covers.
