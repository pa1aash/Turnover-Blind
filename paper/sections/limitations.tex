\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{The placement is not new.} \citet{angelopoulos2023pid} state long-run coverage for any
admissible integrator and any scorecaster and run a Theta model --- an exponential-smoothing
method --- in that slot; \citet{dupuy2026relevance} write the generic argument out in their
Appendix~A; and \citet{duerst2024calibrating} already constrain how a Conformal PID
scorecaster's width may move.

\textbf{We do not claim an exchange relation between the certificate and the movement.} An
earlier draft did. The product of Proposition~2's bound with the integrator's per-step
movement is indeed constant in the horizon and free of the penalty weight, but both factors
are free of the penalty weight by construction --- the bound because the placement leaves it
unchanged, the movement because it is the integrator's own --- so the relation restates the
inheritance rather than pricing it, and it fails to hold on the summed deployed travel for the
tangent integrator of \citet{angelopoulos2023pid}. For the same reason we do not claim that
the integrator's share is beyond the penalty's reach: iteration~(5) admits a scorecaster that
depends on the accumulated error, and such a scorecaster cuts deployed travel by more than two
orders of magnitude when the score distribution is stationary, though the gain falls away when
the budget clip binds. How much of the integrator's movement a bounded scorecaster can offset,
as a function of the budget split and the severity of the shift, is the question this paper
leaves open and does not answer.
\textbf{The inherited guarantee is weak.} With the tangent integrator of
\citet{angelopoulos2023pid} and their own constants, condition~(4) holds with $h(t)=t/\log t$,
so Proposition~2's bound decays as $O(1/\log T)$: at $T=2500$ it certifies realised coverage
only into $[0.821,0.979]$ against a $0.90$ target, and is unchanged for slack $0.01$ to
$0.10$. \textbf{Placement~B changes the producer.} ACI's manipulated variable is $\alpha_t$,
so this replaces ACI with Conformal PID rather than repairing it: \citet{angelopoulos2023pid}
becomes a baseline we must beat, and since they already run a smoothing-family scorecaster
what must be shown narrows to a movement-motivated scorecaster beating an accuracy-motivated
one at matched coverage and width. Turnover measured on an ACI arm does not transfer.
\textbf{The penalty and the measured functional act on different objects.} The penalty shapes
$\hat{q}$ while travel is generated by $q=\hat{q}+r_t(\cdot)$; penalising deployed movement
would need $\hat{q}$ to pre-subtract $r_t$, which the $[-b/2,b/2]$ hypothesis permits only up
to half the integrator's reach. This is Placement~B's sharpest defect; we report travel
decomposed into both shares.

\textbf{Dupuy et al.\ is avoided, not discharged.} Their domination hypothesis --- ``pretty
strong'', ``highly dependent on the choice of parameters'' --- compares partial sums of two
feedback sequences and exists because their Eqs.~(7)--(8) put the smoothed signal inside the
integrator; under Placement~B the integrator's argument is the unmodified indicator, so the
inequality has no referent. We claim avoidance, not discharge, and we do not repair their
Theorem~2. \textbf{Placement~A forfeits the theorem, not coverage.} An EMA of the output
attains $b$ only in the limit of infinitely many consecutive saturated rounds, so
Proposition~2's load-bearing induction step fails and its rate goes with it: at weight
$0.999$ the accumulator reached $623.7$ against a bound of $14.8$. Coverage survived every
smoother we tried --- six families returned $0.1000$--$0.1002$ against $\alpha=0.1$ over
$T=2\times10^5$ --- so reading this paper as showing that Placement~A loses coverage is a
misreading that a ten-minute simulation refutes.

\textbf{The measurement leg is occupied.} \citet{chen2013temporalcorrelation} compare two
scenario sets drawn by Gaussian copula from the same per-period forecast distributions, plot
both gradient curves, and price the difference through a unit commitment carrying
\$300--\$4500 per start with minimum on/off times and ramp rates; an independent 2018
restatement exists, and the mechanism is older still \citep{williams1985lotsizing}; the
nearest single-arm design \citep{delikaraoglou2014scenarios} pairs the same copula construction
with a priced start-up cost but builds only one scenario set.

What is left is the whole of the claim: their design carries no coverage object, so this paper's
matched pair is unavailable in it even in principle; their path functional lives inside one
forecast issue, so a deployed width's revision path is constant across their arms; and their
start-up cost is never attributed to the movement channel in their results.
\textbf{Smaller items.} We exclude the $b=\infty$ branch, legal under Theorem~1 but one on
which $\mathcal{T}_H$ diverges; a learned scorecaster must be clipped to the observed score
range, as the unpenalised method also requires; the exchange binds hardest at short horizons,
a one-point coverage bound forcing $0.072b$ of movement per step at $T=500$ against $0.0036b$
at $T=10^{4}$; and $L_1$ versus $L_2$ is unsettled here.
