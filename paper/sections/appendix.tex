\paragraph{The primary regime.} Every number in Table~\ref{tab:forfeit} is measured under one
deterministic adversary, which consumes no randomness and needs no seed. It plays $s_t = \mathrm{clip}(\tilde{q}_t+\varepsilon,\pm b/2)$ with
$\varepsilon = 10^{-9}$ against the \emph{deployed} threshold, and the indicator is strict. The
specification is not a tie rule, and the distinction is measured rather than argued. Scoring
an exact-threshold play \emph{as a miscoverage} reproduces every quantity
Table~\ref{tab:forfeit} reports: $44$ of $44$ arm-horizon cells identical, all eleven arms at
all four horizons. Scoring
the same play under the \emph{strict} indicator does not, and it moves every row, because the
adversary's signature move then covers rather than misses. Partial adjustment at $w = 0.999$
excurses $70.80$ in place of $623.70$, and the failing dead band returns $0.000000$ in place of
$1.000000$. The $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ limit the harness specifies is the first reading, and
that $623.70$ turns on the adversary's $\varepsilon$, not the counting convention.

\textbf{No row is convention-free with respect to the second}, which is why the adversary is
specified rather than tie-broken. At $\tau = \tau^{\star+}$ itself the two readings part: the
strict indicator returns $0.000000$ where scoring the exact play as a miscoverage returns
$1.000000$. \textbf{Neither of those is coverage.} They are $|E_T| = \alpha T$ and
$(1-\alpha)T$, failures of the same order and opposite sign. Under the strict indicator the
exact-threshold play scores $\mathrm{err}_t = \mathbf{1}\{\tilde{q}_t < -b/2\}$, which is also the
indicator of the constant legal play $s_t \equiv -b/2$, so that column is
Section~\ref{sec:forfeit}'s \emph{mirror adversary} rather than a second convention. It returns
between $0.09992$ and $0.09997$ at $\tau = 0.5$, $0.9$, $0.95$, $0.99$ and $0.999$, and

That locates $\tau^{\star-} = 1$ on the same eleven widths Figure~\ref{fig:boundary} uses, closed

not whether one occurs.

\paragraph{Excursions at $T = 10^4$, and where the excursion law holds.} Partial adjustment
excurses $6.30$, $63.00$ and $623.70$ at $w = 0.9$, $0.99$ and $0.999$. The last two are flat
in $T$ while Proposition~2's bound grows like $\log T$, which is why the ratio falls with the
horizon: at $w = 0.999$ it is $61.08$ at $T = 10^4$ and $42.10$ at $T = 10^6$. On the control and the four partial-adjustment arms the excursion fits
$\max_t|E_t| \approx \max\{0.5\,h(T)+0.9,\ 0.63/(1-w)\}$ at every horizon, most loosely at
$w = 0.9$, the one arm whose two branches cross inside the horizon range. This is a fit to
those five arms, not a derived bound. It does not extend to the other six: the growing time
constants, the running mean and the dead bands all excurse further than it predicts.

\paragraph{What survives of the measurement, and what its status now is.} Outside the admissible
window the transition is a step. Past $\tau^{\star+}$ the threshold sits permanently below the
reachable score range and $|E_t| = (1-\alpha)t$ grows linearly, $60{,}747$ times the bound at $T =
10^6$; the wider bands $\tau = 2.0$, $2.5$, $3.0$ and $5.0$ fail too at $T = 2{\times}10^5$
(Figure~\ref{fig:boundary}). \textbf{Here $\sup_x r_t(x) = b$ and $\hat{q} \equiv 0$, so
$\tau^{\star+} = \tau^{\star-} = b/2$}, one point of the admissible class, and both terms move it.
The legal $\hat{q} \equiv +b/2$ carries the $\tau = 1.5$ band inside the bound against the
specified adversary, and against the mirror one no width covers there, the unsmoothed control
included. Condition~(4) bounds $\sup_x r_t(x)$ only from below, so raising it costs the failure
direction nothing; it moves the edge too (Figure~\ref{fig:settings}) and shrinks the excursion,
from $623.70$ to $20.10$ under the tangent integrator \citet{angelopoulos2023pid} report using in
all their experiments, both at $T = 10^6$. \textbf{The method's own integrator therefore sits
inside the set at every width run here.} \textbf{The endpoint is where the two boundaries part.}
The upper one is closed and the lower one is open, which the null scorecaster cannot show because
$\tau^{\star+}$ and $\tau^{\star-}$ coincide there: four saturators with $\tau^{\star+} <
\tau^{\star-}$ cover at $\tau = \tau^{\star+}$ exactly under both adversaries, and at $\hat{q}
\equiv +0.4$, where $\tau^{\star-} = 0.6 < 1.4 = \tau^{\star+}$, the width $0.599$ covers under
both while $0.6$ fails under the mirror. At the baseline the two coincide at $1$, so the primary
regime covers there and the mirror adversary does not; Figure~\ref{fig:boundary} and this
appendix's primary-regime paragraph print both readings. \textbf{The failing arms have $\mathrm{Sat.} = 1.0000$.} Condition~(4) delivers its full
$\pm b$ on every round, and what fails is Proposition~2's other hypothesis: the threshold closing
the loop \emph{is} the integrator's output.
