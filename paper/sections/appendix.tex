\section{Supporting measurements}
\label{app:support}

\paragraph{The primary regime.} Every number in Table~\ref{tab:forfeit} is measured under one
deterministic adversary, which consumes no randomness and needs no seed. It plays $s_t = \mathrm{clip}(\tilde{q}_t+\varepsilon,\pm b/2)$ with
$\varepsilon = 10^{-9}$ against the \emph{deployed} threshold, and the indicator is strict. The
specification is not a tie rule, and the distinction is measured rather than argued. Scoring
an exact-threshold play \emph{as a miscoverage} reproduces every quantity
Table~\ref{tab:forfeit} reports: $44$ of $44$ arm-horizon cells identical, all eleven arms at
all four horizons. Scoring
the same play under the \emph{strict} indicator does not, and it moves every row, because the
adversary's signature move then covers rather than misses. Partial adjustment at $w = 0.999$
excurses $70.80$ in place of $623.70$, and the failing dead band returns $0.000000$ in place of
$1.000000$. The $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ limit the harness specifies is the first reading.
\textbf{No row is convention-free with respect to the second}, which is why the adversary is
specified rather than tie-broken. At $\tau = \tau^{\star}$ itself the two readings part: the
strict indicator returns $0.000000$ where scoring the exact play as a miscoverage returns
$1.000000$.

\paragraph{Excursions at $T = 10^4$, and where the excursion law holds.} Partial adjustment
excurses $6.30$, $63.00$ and $623.70$ at $w = 0.9$, $0.99$ and $0.999$. The last two are flat
in $T$ while Proposition~2's bound grows like $\log T$, which is why the ratio falls with the
horizon. On the control and the four partial-adjustment arms the excursion fits
$\max_t|E_t| \approx \max\{0.5\,h(T)+0.9,\ 0.63/(1-w)\}$ at every horizon, most loosely at
$w = 0.9$, the one arm whose two branches cross inside the horizon range. This is a fit to
those five arms, not a derived bound. It does not extend to the other six: the growing time
constants, the running mean and the dead bands all excurse further than it predicts.

\paragraph{Realised miscoverage at the covering settings of Figure~\ref{fig:settings}.} At
$\hat{q} \equiv +b/2$ the $\tau = 1.5$ band returns $0.100010$ with $\max_t|E_t| = 11.20$,
inside the bound. Under \citeauthor{angelopoulos2023pid}'s tangent integrator the same width
returns $0.100001$. Raising the saturator's level to $4b$ leaves coverage intact and cuts the
$w = 0.999$ excursion from $623.70$ to $120.60$. That is the first term of $\tau^{\star}$
moving the edge, not the readout changing behaviour.
