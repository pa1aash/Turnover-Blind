\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

An online conformal method emits a threshold before each observation and moves it afterwards.
Forecast stability names that movement, scores it and prices it
\citep{tunc2013nervousness,godahewa2025stability,vanbelle2023deepstability,%
pritularga2024congruence,genov2026switching,vanbelle2026stabilizing}. Varying a forecast's temporal
structure at fixed level was invented earlier, in forecast verification, where the predictive
marginal is matched on real producers \citep{gneiting2007calibration,pinson2012scenarios,worsnop2018scenarios}; that coverage and
mean length under-describe a deployed interval is established too
\citep{min2026questioning,vaze2026simultaneous}. None of it is claimed here.

\paragraph{First: the record carries a claim and its refutation, and this paper joins them.}
Conformal PID \citep{angelopoulos2023pid} adds a \emph{scorecaster} $\hat{q}_{t+1}$ to a saturating
error integrator and asks of it only that it be predictable and lie in $[-b/2,b/2]$ --- ``any function of
the past'' (Theorem~1) --- so a readout placed in that slot is already quantified over. Also in print, of the same
method: ``[s]ince using a scorecaster (D-part) in C-PID breaks the theoretical coverage
guarantee'' \citep{li2024neuralconformal} --- stated and acted on in that paper's baselines
paragraph, refereed and verbatim on printed page $18443$. The refutation is in print too, twice
and with proof:
\citet[Corollary~2]{li2025o2cp} add an arbitrary predictable $d_t$
with $|d_t| \le \mu_t(b/2-|\hat{q}_t|)$ to the deployed threshold, observe that the result ``has
exactly the CPID error-integration form'', and conclude $|E_T| \le c\,h(T)+1 = o(T)$; the same
generic-slot argument carries AcMCP's long-run coverage \citep{wang2026acmcp}. The first and last authors of \citet{li2025o2cp} are the authors
of \citet{li2024neuralconformal} and cite it: the halves already share a bibliography, this is
ordinary self-correction, and the joining is what this paper adds. Four surfaces, queried on 20 August 2026 for the two papers' identifiers and for
``scorecaster'' --- arXiv full text, arXiv abstracts, OpenReview, and the six works Semantic
Scholar records as citing \citet{li2024neuralconformal} --- return no document containing both
the claim and the corollary. \citet{hu2026distinformed} still calls scorecaster choice ``arbitrary'' and
lacking ``principled guidance'', while asserting valid coverage: model selection, not validity. \textbf{We claim neither the placement nor the derivation.}

\paragraph{Second: that admissible set has an edge, and the condition there is tight.} Corollary~2
is a \emph{retention} result, so what it leaves open is outside that set.
Section~\ref{sec:forfeit} exhibits a legal perturbation that leaves it --- an $L_1$ dead band on
the \emph{completed} threshold, the family characterised here --- and locates the edge at $\tau^{\star} = \sup_x r_t(x) + \sup_t \hat{q}_t - b/2$, the corollary's own radius at the
null scorecaster. Past it the price is coverage and not only the rate, so the published sufficient
condition is necessary there. Those two claims are the paper;
Section~\ref{sec:related} sets them beside four vocabularies for the same movement.
