\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A forecast charges a decision twice: once for where it is, and once for how far it moves
between issues, whenever the decision carries an incumbent state that costs something to
revise. The second charge has a literature and none of it is claimed here.
\citet{tunc2013nervousness} price system nervousness in a production plan.
\citet{godahewa2025stability} name forecast stability and publish the one-scalar
partial-adjustment readout $\tilde{y} = (1-w_s)\hat{y}_{\mathrm{new}} + w_s
\tilde{y}_{\mathrm{prev}}$ as model-agnostic post-processing;
\citet{vanbelle2023deepstability,vanbelle2024nbeats} build the objective into deep
forecasters and \citet{caljon2026dynamicloss} weight it against accuracy online;
\citet{pritularga2024congruence} report that accuracy and congruence are only weakly related;
and \citet{genov2026switching} bound a scheduling decision's switching cost by $\beta L_M
\sum_t \|\hat{y}_t - \hat{y}_{t-v}\|$ through a Lipschitz readout map. Closest of all,
\citet{vanbelle2026stabilizing} carry the objective into distribution-free probabilistic
forecasting, pin CRPS across arms and price the residual difference through a newsvendor
charged to revise an incumbent order.

Holding a probabilistic forecast's level fixed and comparing what its temporal structure does
was invented earlier still, in forecast verification. \citet{gneiting2007calibration} state
the governing paradigm --- maximise sharpness subject to calibration --- and that pair is
realised coverage together with mean interval width under its meteorological name.
\citet{pinson2009wpfscenarios} generate scenario sets reproducing a given family of marginal
predictive distributions and differing only in temporal dependence;
\citet{pinson2012scenarios} compare three such sets on a real wind producer, fixing the entire
marginal --- hence coverage and mean width exactly, a stronger control than the pair --- and
varying only rank dependence; \citet{worsnop2018scenarios} compare sets carrying the same
quantiles and the same spread. That literature also
commissions the step it declines to take: \citet{pinson2012scenarios} conclude that ``a more
intuitive approach to the evaluation of sets of scenarios may be to concentrate on their
value instead, i.e.\ on the comparative benefits from their use as input to various
decision-making problems''.

What an online conformal producer adds is narrow and we state it exactly. The \emph{object}
differs: those arms vary the copula within a single forecast issue, so the calendar-time path
of the deployed width is the same in every arm by construction, whereas what is varied here
is the revision path of one deployed interval across successive issues. The \emph{producer}
differs: an online conformal interval's width path is generated by the calibration rule's own
feedback, making it a tunable of the method rather than a property of the data. And the
matched pair is not an arbitrary level functional --- realised coverage and mean interval
width are the two quantities on which ACI, DtACI, SAOCP and Conformal PID are reported and
tuned \citep{gibbs2021aci,gibbs2024dtaci,bhatnagar2023saocp,angelopoulos2023pid} --- so
fixing both at once is a statement about a field's tuning practice. That the pair is together
insufficient is not our observation: \citet{min2026questioning} and
\citet{vaze2026simultaneous} make it first, and \citet{srinivas2026efficiency} and
\citet{zhou2025inverse} trade coverage against efficiency along an explicit curve. We fix both and add
one measurement, the deployed threshold's \emph{travel} $\mathcal{T}_H = \sum_{t \le H} |q_t
- q_{t-1}|$ (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

The summed functional is already published as a named forecast metric \citep{zanotti2026smqc};
the name is introduced for its deployed-conformal reading, not for the arithmetic.

Reducing travel calls for a one-scalar movement penalty on the deployed quantile, and that
object is published too --- \citet{godahewa2025stability} in the point-forecast readout, and
\citet{binny2025whomoved} on a deployed conformal calibration threshold as $q \leftarrow
(1-\gamma)q + \gamma\hat{q}$. The live question is where in the calibration loop the penalty
sits, because that decides which existing validity argument still covers the interval
actually deployed. Placed in the \emph{scorecaster slot} of Conformal PID
\citep{angelopoulos2023pid}, both the $L_2$ (convex-combination) and $L_1$ (soft-threshold)
penalties are convex combinations of quantities the theorem already bounds, so its Theorem~1
applies unmodified, its constant is unchanged, and long-run coverage is inherited
deterministically. \textbf{That placement is not new and we claim nothing for it}:
\citet{angelopoulos2023pid} state long-run coverage ``for any choice of integrator $r_t$ that
satisfies the appropriate saturation condition, and any scorecaster $\hat{q}_{t+1}$'' and run
a Theta-model scorecaster themselves, \citet{dupuy2026relevance} publish the generic argument
in their Appendix~A, and \citet{duerst2024calibrating} already constrain a Conformal
PID scorecaster's width path.

\paragraph{Contributions.} \textbf{(i)} Smoothing the \emph{completed} output of a quantile
tracker forfeits the inherited theorem and its finite-sample rate: under an exponential filter
of weight $0.999$ the accumulator reached $623.7$ against a Proposition~2 bound of $14.8$, and
the forfeit grows in exactly the time constant a movement-averse designer raises. The failing
step is the induction's ``saturated at $T$ implies covered at $T$'', not the saturation
condition, which constrains the integrator alone and which a downstream filter never touches.
\textbf{(ii)} Long-run coverage nonetheless survives that placement --- six filter families
return miscoverage $0.1000$--$0.1002$ against $\alpha = 0.1$ over $T = 2 \times 10^5$ --- so
the price of smoothing downstream is the certificate and its rate, and we claim nothing about
coverage loss. \textbf{(iii)} Placing the penalty in the scorecaster slot instead forfeits
nothing, which corrects a claim in print that doing so ``breaks the theoretical coverage
guarantee'' \citep{li2024neuralconformal}: iteration~(5) quantifies over any function of the
past, and both penalty forms are convex combinations of quantities already in range.
\textbf{(iv)} On a real producer we report coverage, mean width, travel and the net log growth
of a position charged to move, the first two matched across arms.
