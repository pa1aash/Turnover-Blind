\begin{abstract}
Conformal PID states long-run coverage for an arbitrary bounded scorecaster, and
a published corollary extends it to any predictable perturbation of the deployed
threshold inside an admissible set; a contrary reading is in print, by the same
authors, and is acted on. We join the halves and claim neither. What we add is a
measurement outside that set: a one-scalar movement penalty on the
\emph{completed} threshold forfeits Proposition~2's rate by $42\times$ at $T =
10^{6}$ and, past a measurable width, returns realised miscoverage $1.000000$
against $\alpha = 0.1$ while the saturation condition fires on every round. That
width is $\tau^{\star} = \sup r_t + \sup\hat{q}_t - b/2$, exactly the
corollary's admissible radius at the null scorecaster---so the conceded result
is tight there, and leaving the set costs coverage rather than the rate. Under a
legal non-zero scorecaster, or Conformal PID's own unbounded integrator, it does
not.
\end{abstract}
